# Positioning & Visibility: getting found, not just applying

## The one idea (and the honest tension with volume)

Top-tier opportunities often go to people who were already being watched before they applied. A
strong public track record, combined with a handful of real referral relationships, tends to beat raw
application volume for the roles that matter most to you. That isn't a claim that applying doesn't
work; it's a claim that being visible changes which door you walk through when you do.

The honest tension: visibility takes longer to pay off than mass-applying does. A portfolio, a body
of public work, and real relationships get built over months, not the afternoon before you need a
job. Most people should do both at once, not pick one: keep applying on the timeline you actually
need an outcome on, and build visibility in parallel for the timeline after that. See `CLAUDE.md` §5
for the evidence behind ranking a referral above volume.

## Bottom line

Visibility is a slower, higher-ceiling lever than application volume, and it compounds: a real
portfolio, a real post, a real relationship all still exist the next time you need them, while a
submitted application doesn't. Build it in parallel with your actual search, not instead of it, and
don't let "I should build my portfolio first" become a reason to delay applying to something real
today.
