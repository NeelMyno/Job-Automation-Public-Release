# Strengths & Market Position — the adversarial self-audit

## The method

Run this as an audit that tries to refute your own rarity claims, not confirm them. The goal isn't a
flattering list — it's the small number of claims that survive you actively trying to break them.

1. **List everything you think makes you stand out.** Skills, combinations of skills, experience,
   scale, domain knowledge — whatever comes to mind. Don't filter yet.
2. **For each one, grade it:** Real? / Rare? / Would someone pay specifically for this combination?
   (the table below).
3. **Cut anything that doesn't survive.** Not real: it's a lie, cut it. Real but common: it's a
   baseline expectation, not a differentiator. Real and rare but nobody would pay for the specific
   combination: it's a hobby, not a positioning asset.
4. **Name the ONE thing that's genuinely rare** about your specific combination of skills and
   experience — not a list of five, one. If you can't get down to one, you haven't cut hard enough
   yet.

## Known bias in your own self-assessment

Everyone overrates the thing they worked hardest on and underrates the thing that came easily. The
skill that took years of deliberate effort feels like it should count for more than the one that was
always just natural — and that instinct runs backward from how a market actually prices things: rare
and easy for you is worth more than common and hard-won. When you grade the table above, check
yourself specifically for this. Ask what you're underrating just because it never felt like work.
